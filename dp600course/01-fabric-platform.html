Deep dive: what "SaaS" means here
  • Spark, SQL, KQL and Analysis Services engines are provisioned for you; you only pick sizes and settings.
  • OneLake storage is automatic and billed per GB, separately from compute.
  • Every workload consumes CUs from the capacity its workspace is assigned to.
  • Tenant settings, domains, labels, endorsement, lineage and audit apply across all workloads (admin portal + Microsoft Purview).
  • Data Science (ML models, experiments) is light on DP-600. Copilot and the Fabric data agent (formerly AI skill) span workloads. New workloads keep arriving (Workload Hub); the exam focuses on the ones above.

Deep dive: APIs, shortcuts, security, billing
  • HTTPS form: https://onelake.dfs.fabric.microsoft.com/<workspace>/<item>.<itemtype>/Files/…. Names or GUIDs work.
  • Azure Storage Explorer, Databricks, Synapse and AzCopy can read/write with Entra ID auth.

  • OneLake catalog "Govern" tab gives owners insights (unlabeled items, missing descriptions); "Secure" view shows security roles.

Deep dive: buying, scaling, pausing, P SKUs, Spark
  • F SKUs are Azure resources (Microsoft Fabric capacity) in a subscription and region; you name capacity admins. Billing per second (1-minute minimum) pay-as-you-go, or a cheaper 1-year reservation.
  • Scale up/down any time, no data movement. Larger SKUs F4096 and F8192 also exist.
  • Pause stops compute billing; content is unavailable, storage still billed. Pausing bills any smoothed future usage.
  • P SKUs run Fabric too but are being retired for new purchases; EM and A SKUs don't support Fabric items.
  • PPU is not a Fabric capacity: no lakehouse, notebook etc. PPU adds XMLA, larger models, 48 refreshes/day.
  • Trials: tenant setting "Users can try Microsoft Fabric paid features".
  • Spark: 1 CU = 2 Spark VCores (F64 = 128) plus a burst factor for concurrent jobs. Starter pool = pre-warmed, starts in seconds; custom pools start slower. High concurrency lets notebooks share one session.

Deep dive: overage mechanics and extra controls
  • Timepoint = 30-second evaluation interval. Smoothed usage above 100 % = overage; unpaid overage = carryforward, paid back by burndown.
  • Interactive = report/DAX queries and most user-triggered requests. Background = refreshes, pipelines, Spark, almost all warehouse operations.
  • Utilization spikes over 100 % in the Metrics app mean overage, not automatically throttling.
  • Real-Time Intelligence skips the 20-second delay stage and starts at rejection.
  • Error users see: CapacityLimitExceeded / "Your organization's Fabric compute capacity has exceeded its limits".
  • Capacity overage billing: pay extra instead of being throttled. Surge protection: reject new background jobs above a threshold to protect interactive users.
  • Autoscale Billing for Spark: Spark runs on separate pay-as-you-go billing; no bursting/smoothing for it.
  • Throttling is per capacity: an overloaded capacity never slows another one. Admin monitoring workspace = tenant-level adoption reports.
  1. Overage protection (≤ 10 min)
  2. Interactive delay (10–60 min)
  3. Interactive rejection (60 min–24 h)
  4. Background rejection (> 24 h)

Exam traps

  • There is no "OneLake per workspace" and no storage account to create.
  • The lakehouse SQL analytics endpoint is read-only: T-SQL writes need a warehouse.
  • PPU is not a Fabric capacity; free viewers need F64+, not F32.
  • Bursting and smoothing are automatic; throttling starts after 10 minutes of overage.
  • Domains and labels don't control residency; the capacity region does.
