Exam guide & skills outline

Everything about the DP-600 exam itself: format, question types, scoring, renewal, the current three-domain skills outline (revised 2026-04-20) mapped skill-by-skill to the pages of this portal, what changed compared with older outlines, a study plan and exam-day tactics.

The exam at a glance

Exam DP-600: Implementing Analytics Solutions Using Microsoft Fabric earns the Microsoft Certified: Fabric Analytics Engineer Associate credential. It is a role-based, associate-level exam. There are no formal prerequisites, but Microsoft describes the candidate as someone who designs, creates and deploys enterprise-scale analytics solutions and is comfortable with SQL, DAX and PySpark, data modeling, data transformation and Git-based source control.

AspectWhat to expect
DurationAbout 100 minutes of answering time (plan for roughly 120 minutes of seat time including the NDA, instructions and the optional survey). Non-native speakers can request +30 minutes as an accommodation when the exam is not offered in their language.
Number of questionsTypically 40–60 items; the exact number varies per exam form. One or more case studies (each with several questions) are usually part of the exam.
Passing score700 on a scale of 1–1000. The score is scaled, not a percentage: 700 does not mean 70 % correct, and items can carry different weights.
Scoring rulesNo negative marking – never leave an item blank. Multi-part items (for example "select two" or hot-area items) may award partial credit per correct part. Some unscored "beta" items may be included; you cannot tell which.
DeliveryPearson VUE test center or online proctored (OnVUE). Online: clean desk, webcam, ID, no second monitor.
Open bookMicrosoft role-based exams allow access to Microsoft Learn in a split-screen window during the exam (the exam timer keeps running while you search). Use it only to confirm a fact, never to learn a topic – it is slow.
Preview featuresMost questions cover generally available (GA) features, but Microsoft states that commonly used preview features can appear.
PriceRegional; USD 165 in the United States (discounts for students and some programs).
Retake policyWait 24 hours after the first failed attempt, then 14 days between subsequent attempts; maximum 5 attempts in 12 months.
Validity & renewalThe certification is valid for 1 year. Renew for free with an unproctored online renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn; the renewal window opens 6 months (180 days) before expiration and you can retry it.
PracticeMicrosoft offers a free Practice Assessment (new experience on AI Skills Navigator, plus the legacy one on Learn) and an exam sandbox demo of the exam UI.

Exam length, number of items and question types are not fixed by contract – Microsoft varies them between exam forms. Treat the numbers above as typical ranges, not guarantees.

Question types and how to handle them

TypeDescriptionTactics
Multiple choice (single answer)One correct option out of 4 (sometimes more).Eliminate options that break a hard constraint (license, role, "without copying data", "least effort").
Multiple response"Select two/three" – each correct choice is often a complete solution or part of one.Read whether each answer is a complete solution or part of the solution.
Drag and drop / build listMatch items to targets or put steps in the correct order (e.g. deployment steps, T-SQL statements to build RLS).Not all options are used; some may be used more than once.
Hot areaPick values in drop-downs inside a code snippet or a screenshot (e.g. complete a DAX measure, a KQL query, a PySpark write).Know syntax precisely: CALCULATE/REMOVEFILTERS, summarize ... by, .mode("overwrite"), MERGE etc.
Yes/No series ("Does this meet the goal?")Same scenario repeated with a different proposed solution. Once answered you cannot return.Judge each proposal independently; more than one can be "Yes".
Case studyMulti-tab scenario (overview, existing environment, requirements, technical requirements, problem statements) followed by several questions. Once you leave a case study section you cannot go back to it.Read the questions first, then scan the tabs for the matching requirement. Requirements words like "minimize administrative effort", "must not copy data", "users must not be able to query the lakehouse directly" decide the answer.
Lab-style / scenario tasksSome Microsoft exams include interactive or performance-based components. If they appear, they test practical steps such as configuring a workspace setting or completing code.Hands-on practice in a Fabric trial is the only preparation that works.

Exam questions are written around constraints. Underline the qualifiers: "least privilege", "minimize cost", "without duplicating data", "near real-time", "must be able to use T-SQL", "the solution must not require a Pro license". Usually two options are technically possible and only one meets every qualifier.

How the domains feel on the exam

Maintain a data analytics solution

  • Lots of "which role / which permission / least privilege" questions: workspace roles vs item permissions (Read, ReadData, ReadAll, Build, Write, Reshare), OneLake security roles, SQL GRANT/DENY, RLS/CLS/OLS, dynamic data masking.
  • Governance: sensitivity labels and inheritance, endorsement (Promoted, Certified, Master data), domains, lineage and impact analysis.
  • Lifecycle: Git integration (Azure DevOps / GitHub), branching, .pbip/.pbir/TMDL, deployment pipelines with deployment rules, database projects (SQL projects / .sqlproj / DACPAC), XMLA endpoint read-write, .pbit/.pbids, shared semantic models.

Prepare data (the largest domain)

  • Choosing an ingestion tool: pipeline Copy activity vs Copy job vs Dataflow Gen2 vs notebook vs shortcut vs mirroring vs eventstream.
  • Choosing a store: lakehouse vs warehouse vs eventhouse (and SQL database).
  • Transformations in T-SQL, PySpark/Spark SQL and Power Query: star schemas, SCD Type 1/2, deduplication, null handling, type conversion.
  • Querying with SQL, the visual query editor, KQL and DAX – expect code completion items.

Implement and manage semantic models

  • Storage modes and Direct Lake details (framing, fallback, guardrails, Direct Lake on OneLake vs on SQL endpoint).
  • Modeling: star schema, relationships, many-to-many, bridge tables, composite models, Dual mode, aggregations.
  • DAX: variables, iterators, CALCULATE and filter functions, window functions (OFFSET, INDEX, WINDOW), information functions, calculation groups, dynamic format strings, field parameters.
  • Performance: Performance Analyzer, DAX Studio (server timings, VertiPaq Analyzer), Tabular Editor (Best Practice Analyzer), incremental refresh, large semantic model storage format.

Exam-taking tips

Time management

  • Budget ≈ 1.5–2 minutes per standalone question and ≈ 15–20 minutes per case study. Note the remaining time when you start a case study.
  • Use Mark for review for long items – but remember Yes/No series and finished case studies can't be revisited.
  • Don't burn time in Microsoft Learn: search only for a precise fact (e.g. a T-SQL option name) and only when you have time left.

Decoding questions

  • "Least privilege" → choose the narrowest scope: item permission over workspace role, Viewer over Contributor, ReadData over workspace membership, Build only where report creation is needed.
  • "Without copying/duplicating data" → shortcuts, mirroring (a managed replica, but no pipeline you build), Direct Lake, OneLake availability for eventhouse.
  • "Minimize development effort / low-code" → Copy job or Dataflow Gen2 over notebooks; built-in features over custom code.
  • "T-SQL / multi-table transactions / stored procedures that write" → warehouse (the lakehouse SQL analytics endpoint is read-only).
  • "Spark / Python / unstructured files" → lakehouse. "Streaming telemetry, logs, time series, KQL" → eventhouse.
  • "Users must not access the source data directly" → semantic model with a fixed identity connection and model RLS; don't give lakehouse permissions.
  • "Free-license users must view reports" → capacity F64 or larger (or P SKU) and a Viewer role / app access.

Common traps

  • Contributors, Members and Admins are not restricted by semantic model RLS (they have Write on the model). Test with Viewers.
  • Promoting needs Write permission; certifying needs to be in the group named in the tenant setting.
  • Direct Lake on SQL endpoint falls back to DirectQuery; Direct Lake on OneLake does not fall back – it errors.
  • Deployment pipelines move metadata, not lakehouse/warehouse data.
  • Bursting and smoothing are automatic; throttling begins only after the 10-minute overage protection is exhausted.

Knowledge checks

What is the weight of the "Prepare data" domain in the outline revised on 2026-04-20?

  • 10–15 %
  • 25–30 %
  • 45–50 %
  • 40–45 %

Prepare data is 45–50 %, by far the largest domain. 40–45 % was "Prepare and serve data" in the old four-domain outline; 25–30 % applies to the other two current domains; 10–15 % was the old "Plan, implement and manage" domain.

Your DP-600 certification expires on 30 June. What must you do to keep it?

  • Retake the full proctored exam before 30 June
  • Pass the free online renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn, available from about six months before expiry
  • Nothing – associate certifications don't expire
  • Pay a renewal fee every two years

Associate certifications are valid for one year and are renewed with a free, unproctored renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn that opens 180 days before expiration. No retake or fee is needed.

Which statement about the passing score is correct?

  • You need 70 % of questions correct
  • You need 700 correct points out of exactly 1000 equal-weight points
  • Wrong answers subtract points, so skipping is safer
  • The passing score is a scaled 700, and blank answers are simply scored as wrong

Microsoft uses a scaled score with 700 as the pass mark; it is not a simple percentage and items can carry different weights. There is no negative marking, so always answer every item.

In a case study, you answered all questions and moved to the next section. You now want to change one answer. What happens?

  • You can't – once you leave a case study section, you can't return to it
  • You can return via the review screen at the end of the exam
  • You can return only if you marked the question for review
  • You can return but only to read, not to change answers

Case studies are separate sections. You can review questions inside the case study before leaving it, but after you finish the section it is locked. The same "no return" rule applies to Yes/No series questions.

A blog post lists "Create a custom Power BI report theme" and "Integrate prescriptive and predictive analytics into a visual" as DP-600 skills. What should you conclude?

  • These are core skills of the current outline
  • The post is based on the original 2024 four-domain outline; these bullets are no longer listed
  • These skills moved to Domain 3 in 2026
  • These skills are tested only in case studies

Report themes and exploratory/predictive analytics were part of the launch outline. The three-domain outline (from November 2024, refreshed 2026-04-20) dropped them in favour of security, governance, eventhouse/KQL and Direct Lake topics.

A question asks for the solution that gives an analyst access with "least privilege". Which option pattern usually wins?

  • Add the analyst to the workspace Member role so all needs are covered
  • Make the analyst a capacity administrator
  • Grant the narrowest item-level permission (for example ReadData or Build on a single item) that satisfies the requirement
  • Grant Contributor so the analyst can test RLS

"Least privilege" signals the narrowest scope: item permissions rather than workspace roles, and only the specific permission needed (Read, ReadData, ReadAll or Build). Member, Contributor and capacity admin rights all go beyond the requirement.

Practice scenario

You have eight weeks before your exam and strong Power BI skills (PL-300 level) but little SQL or Spark experience. Your colleague suggests spending most of your time on DAX and report design "because that's what you know best and DP-600 is a Power BI exam". How should you plan instead?

Weight your plan by the outline, not by comfort. Prepare data is 45–50 % and is your weakest area: spend roughly half of the time on ingestion choices (pipeline, Copy job, Dataflow Gen2, notebook, shortcut, mirroring), lakehouse/Delta/Spark, warehouse T-SQL (views, procedures, SCD), KQL and querying. Report design is barely tested any more (report themes were removed). For semantic models, focus on the parts PL-300 does not cover deeply: Direct Lake (framing, fallback, Direct Lake on OneLake vs SQL endpoint), composite models, calculation groups, XMLA/Tabular Editor/DAX Studio and incremental refresh. Reserve the final weeks for security/governance/lifecycle (25–30 %) and timed practice. Use a Fabric trial for hands-on work every week.

Key takeaways & exam tips

  • DP-600: ≈100 minutes, ≈40–60 items, case studies included, pass mark 700 (scaled), no penalty for guessing, one-year validity with a free renewal assessment.
  • Current outline (revised 2026-04-20): Maintain 25–30 %, Prepare data 45–50 %, Implement and manage semantic models 25–30 %.
  • Older material with four domains, report themes or "explore and analyze" analytics is outdated – but most of its technical content is still useful.
  • Learn current names: Activator, OneLake catalog, OneLake security, Fabric data agent, Copy job, Dataflow Gen2, Direct Lake on OneLake vs on SQL endpoint, workspace type.
  • Read each scenario for constraints (least privilege, no data copies, low code, T-SQL required, free-license viewers) – they decide between two plausible answers.
  • Case studies and Yes/No series can't be revisited; budget time before entering them.
  • Hands-on practice in a trial capacity is the single best predictor of success – every page of this portal suggests what to build.
